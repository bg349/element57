Why phosphate matters

Chlorine kills algae. It doesn't take away its food.

Algae needs sunlight, warm water and nutrients. The nutrient most pools have too much of is phosphate.

When phosphate is high, algae comes back the moment chlorine runs low after a hot weekend or a storm. Shock clears it, the phosphate stays, and the cycle repeats.

Lanthanum chloride breaks the cycle. It binds phosphate into a solid your filter removes, so there's far less left for algae to grow on.

How it works

Watch a green pool come back

Keep scrolling. The water behind this page goes through the same recovery your pool would.

01 · The problem

A green pool

Algae is thriving on phosphate. Test first: a reading over a few hundred ppb means algae has plenty to feed on.

02 · Shock

Kill the algae with chlorine

Shock does the killing. The water turns dull and cloudy as dead algae hangs in it, but the phosphate is still there.

03 · Treat

Add Element 57

With the pump running, pour it in front of a return jet. Lanthanum binds phosphate (La³⁺ + PO₄³⁻ → LaPO₄), and the water turns briefly milky as that solid forms.

04 · Filter

Let the filter do the rest

Run the filter until the haze clears. Vacuum anything that settles, then clean or backwash the filter.

Cartridge filters catch the solid fast, so check them more often during treatment.

05 · Clear

Clear, with nothing to feed on

Retest. With phosphate under 100 ppb and chlorine kept in range, algae has very little to grow back on.

Questions

Good questions

Does this replace chlorine?

No. Chlorine or another sanitizer keeps the water safe to swim in. Removing phosphate takes away algae's food, so your chlorine isn't constantly fighting new growth.

Why did my water turn cloudy?

That haze is phosphate being bound into lanthanum phosphate, a fine solid. It means the product is working. Run the filter and it clears.

Will it kill the algae already in my pool?

No. Shock and brush first to kill existing algae, then use the phosphate remover so it has less to grow back on.

Can I swim after adding it?

Follow the swim time on the label. [Add the label's wait time here.]

How often should I use it?

Test phosphate every week or two. Treat when the reading climbs, and after heavy rain, a lot of leaves or a refill.

Is it safe for salt pools?

Lanthanum chloride phosphate removers are widely used in saltwater pools. Follow the label for your system.
